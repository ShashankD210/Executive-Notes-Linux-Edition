# IT Audit Operating Model

## Audit universe
Governance and IT strategy; architecture/change management; identity and access; operations; infrastructure; cloud; cybersecurity; SDLC/DevSecOps; data protection; third parties; business continuity; regulatory compliance; system hardening.

## Lifecycle
1. Plan: define objective, scope, period, criteria, systems, locations and independence.
2. Risk assess: consider inherent risk, prior findings, incidents, material changes and third parties.
3. Test design: determine whether control design addresses the stated risk.
4. Test operating effectiveness: sample evidence across the audit period; document population, selection, procedure and result.
5. Evaluate: distinguish design deficiency, operating failure, scope limitation and observation.
6. Report: condition, criteria, cause, consequence/risk, recommendation, owner and due date.
7. Follow up: validate remediation evidence and independently close findings.

## Evidence standards
Evidence must be relevant, reliable, sufficient, attributable and time-bounded. Record source system, extraction date, population, sample, tester, test procedure, result and secure reference. Preserve chain of custody where required. Avoid storing unnecessary sensitive raw data.

## Finding severity
Use the organization's approved methodology. At minimum assess impact, likelihood, exposure, control gap, exploitability, regulatory impact and compensating controls. Severity labels must map to response SLAs approved by risk governance.

## Independence
Internal audit must not own control operation, risk acceptance or remediation implementation. Management owns controls and corrective action; audit validates closure.
